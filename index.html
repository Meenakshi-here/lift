How it measures, and the limits used

The accelerometer reads the force on the lift floor about 60 times a second. The app turns that into the lift's vertical motion in five steps:

  1. Find "up". While the phone is still, the average reading is gravity. Projecting every sample onto it gives vertical acceleration, however the phone lies. The rest is sideways motion.
  2. Remove sensor offset. The still periods before and after each trip give the sensor's offset, which is subtracted along the trip.
  3. Filter with zero phase lag. Motion: 3 Hz low-pass. Jerk: derivative of a 2 Hz low-pass. Vibration: 1–25 Hz band (less if the phone samples slower). Filters run forward and backward so peaks keep their timing.
  4. Integrate with zero-velocity correction. Acceleration is integrated to speed and height. The lift is stopped at both ends, so leftover speed is drift and is removed. This keeps travel within a few percent.
  5. Score the ride in the style of ISO 18738-1: peak start acceleration and stop deceleration, peak jerk, and A95 peak-to-peak vibration (the level 95% of 0.5 s windows stay under) in milli-g, measured at steady speed where the lift reaches it.

Limits are typical passenger-lift comfort guidance; ISO 18738 defines how to measure, not pass/fail values. Phone sensors have a noise floor of a few milli-g, shown under each report. This is a screening tool for spotting a lift that needs attention, not a certified ride-quality analyser.
